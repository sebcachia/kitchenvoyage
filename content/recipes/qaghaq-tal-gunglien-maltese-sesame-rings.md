*Qagħaq tal-Ġunġlien* literally translates to sesame rings. These are traditional Maltese biscuits made from wheat flour, lemon and orange peel and a bunch of aromatics, including cinnamon, cloves and anise seed. You will also find them shaped in a figure of eight and called *Ottijiet *(*otto* = eight in Italian). They are commonly found all year round in bakeries, confectioneries and supermarkets all over the Maltese Islands.  


The sesame rings are crunchy and full of aroma and are a great accompaniment to a hot cup of tea or coffee. These sesame rings divide those who love them in 2 groups... Those who just eat them as they are or those who dip them in their tea/coffee. I admit that I belong to the latter group. In other cases, I do love a good crunch on a biscuit but I find that the sesame rings are just meant to be dipped in the tea and let them melt in your mouth. Maybe it's a childhood memory and I just can't do otherwise! :D


Here in Budapest, it is kind of difficult to find any sesame rings. So I asked my mum whether she has a recipe for them so I would also enjoy them while away from home. 


And here they are! I am really happy with my first attempt and will definitely make them again in the future! :D
